Language Educator & Designer of Engaging Learning Experiences

I'm Austin Pack, Assistant Professor of English Language Teaching & Learning at BYU–Hawaii. I study how thoughtful use of generative AI tools can deepen language learning instead of shortcutting it. Some recent example projects are highlighted below.

Recent projects

AI tools empower language educators to create websites and edtech platforms that address their students' needs. The projects highlighted below were created to address the needs of my own students, such as written corrective feedback, comprehensible input, personalized materials, and spaced repetition.

Automatic Written Corrective Feedback

AI provides written corrective feedback in the form of error codes, letting students decide how to fix the errors themselves.

Students write multiple drafts on a weekly prompt. AI marks each error with a code instead of correcting it and builds worksheets targeting students’ most common errors. AI-powered data analytics provide language teachers with real-time insights into the grammatical needs of their students.

Hawaiʻi Postcards and vocabulary workbook

A calm pixel-art game providing contextualized academic vocabulary practice.

Students visit a new place in Hawaiʻi each week and practice ten academic words chosen for their major from the New Academic Word List. The companion workbook gives each word its family, parts and collocations, with practice, spaced review and a progress page.

Used in
English for academic purposes classes at BYU–Hawaii

13 places, ten words a week, chosen for each of BYU–Hawaii’s 25 majors

Academic Listening Practice Lab

Guided academic listening practice with eighteen TED talks and sixteen listening strategies.

A course website for B1 academic listening. Each lesson teaches one strategy and has students listen for different purposes, checks their answers against the transcript, and ends with academic word practice and a reflection. No accounts: progress is saved in the browser and can be exported.

Sixty Radicals and the Mandarin Tone Trainer

Learn the sixty radicals that matter most, and every new character arrives with a clue.

Stroke-drawn lessons, radical hunts through texts students would actually come across, character building, and a 60-second decode sprint, with Mandarin audio throughout. The Tone Trainer helps students distinguish between tones: hear a syllable, name its tone, then move on to tone pairs and see which tones you mix up.

For
beginning learners of Mandarin
Covers
60 radicals, 20 sound families, 4 tones and their pairs

Spanish language and culture

Practice Spanish by getting yourself around Valencia, Spain, and Mexico City, Mexico, one conversation at a time.

In Valencia you travel from the airport to the EUROCALL conference through twenty-four voiced conversations, then read the city’s history at ten real sites. Tap any word for a gloss; phrases you meet come back in a spaced-repetition notebook. The same engine runs a semester abroad in Mexico City.

Levels
A2 to B2
Varieties

LIFT Lab

Language Innovation and Futures in TESOL: a mobile lab in the BYU–Hawaii TESOL program where faculty and preservice teachers use AI agents to build educational technology and teaching materials. The aim is to empower pre-service teachers to use AI in ways grounded in evidence-based principles of learning.

AI agents for differentiated materials

Tiered texts that include scaffolded tasks and culturally adapted content are too expensive to produce at scale for publishers. This project studies agentic AI as an authoring partner that could put differentiated materials within a teacher's reach.

About

I am an Assistant Professor of English Language Teaching and Learning at Brigham Young University–Hawaii. My work sits where language education, motivation research and the responsible and effective use of artificial intelligence meet.

I'm mainly interested in how generative AI in language education can assist with automated feedback, differentiated materials, and learner-facing tools. The question I keep returning to is how AI can be used so that learners do more of the thinking, not less.

My research has focused on leveraging generative AI and large language models for language education, language pedagogy, computer-assisted language learning, the psychology of language learning, complex dynamic systems, and VR learning environments.
