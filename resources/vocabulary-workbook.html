    Where your ten words come from

    Every word in this workbook is from the New Academic Word List (NAWL) — 963 words chosen by researchers because they appear again and again in university textbooks, lectures, and journal articles across every field. They are the words that do the heavy lifting in academic English but rarely show up in everyday conversation.

    What makes your list yours is the order. The same 963 words are re-sorted for your major, so the words that matter most in your field rise to the top and become your earliest weeks.

    How the order is decided

    1
    Every word already has a relevance score for every major.

    Each NAWL word was rated from 0 to 1 for each of the 25 majors, where 1 means “very likely needed in that major’s coursework.” The ratings come from sorting each word into a subject area (medical, statistics, linguistics, economics, general academic, and so on), giving each area a score for every major, and then hand-adjusting the roughly 170 words that cross fields — notation, for example, scores 1 for both Mathematics and Music.

    2
    When you pick a major, all the words are re-sorted using two keys.

    First key — relevance. Words scored 1 for your major come before words scored 0.75, which come before 0.5, and so on.
    Tie-breaker — frequency. Within a band of equal scores, the more common academic word comes first. So for Accounting, obtain (very common) appears before transaction (less common), even though both score 1.

    How relevant is it to my field? decides the band; how common is it in academic English? decides the order inside the band.

    3
    The sorted list is cut into courses and weeks.

    Words 1–140 become Academic English 1 (ten a week for fourteen weeks); words 141–280 become Academic English 2. Words past 280 are the low-relevance remainder for that major and are never shown — for Accounting, that is words like chromosome.

    Two things worth knowing

    Week 1 looks similar across majors; later weeks diverge. The earliest words are the ones that are both highly relevant and highly frequent, and a handful of words rated 1 for everyone — obtain, aspect, impact, valid — sit near the top of every major’s list. Specialist vocabulary enters as soon as your field’s words start outranking the general ones.
    Science majors get a different experience. Because science vocabulary dominates the NAWL, a Biology student’s entire two-semester program is made of top-rated words in pure frequency order — and still never runs out of them.
